# Game Boy theme

The sea as the original 1989 Game Boy (DMG) would draw it, composed the way real DMG games do (Link's
Awakening, Pokemon Gold/Crystal, Wario Land): big FLAT tone areas, clean one-tile transitions and
well-drawn sprites, never noise fields. It is meant to be looked at for hours, so it is calm: the
background hardly moves and the creatures glide.

Everything is generated by `tools/gen-gameboy-theme.js` (run `node tools/gen-gameboy-theme.js`; `--check`
is part of `npm test`; `--sheet out.png 8` writes a contact sheet of all sprites).

## Emulated constraints

| DMG hardware | here |
|---|---|
| 4 LCD shades | exactly four colours in every texture: `#0f380f #306230 #8bac0f #9bbc0f`, the pea-green set used by DMG emulators (darkest ink to lightest "off" pixel). Nothing is blended or computed at run time. |
| 8x8 tiles | all art is cut from 8x8 tiles. The generator counts unique tiles (flips folded) and fails the build above DMG limits: OBJ <= 256, BG <= 256, both <= 384 (VRAM). Currently OBJ 151, BG 187. |
| OBJ metasprites | whole tiles: fish 16x8 (S/M) and 16x16 (L), fry and larva 8x8, angler 24x16, orca 40x16, jelly 16x16 and 8x16, worm 8x8, bubbles 8x8, lure and sparkle 8x8. |
| OBJ palettes: 3 shades + transparent | every sprite uses at most three shades (asserted at build and by the tests). Fish "colours" are shade patterns (plain, bars, dorsal stripe, flank stripe, spots), not hues. |
| Palette registers (BGP/OBP) | the same art is stored once per register mapping as a small atlas: BG day / dusk / night fades (`bg-*.png`), OBJ normal / dimmed / inverted (`obj-n0/n1/i0.png`). Changing palette = picking another texture. |
| Integer-scaled LCD | the scene is a virtual screen of about 360 rows (640x360 on a 16:9 screen) scaled by a whole number of physical pixels (3x at 1080p, 2x at 720p, 6x at 4K); one art texel scale for every sprite. |

Deliberately not emulated: the 10-sprites-per-scanline limit and the 40-sprite OAM cap (they would flicker), the LCD pixel grid (a faint grid over the whole screen reads as noise).

## Composition (what the owner asked for)

* **Water**: three flat zones. A lit top layer (shade 2, 15 % of the height) with a surface line of foam and a few wide, flat, lightest-shade light shafts; open water (shade 1, 57 %); deep water (shade 0). Each tone change is one 3-row dither strip, static. The lightest shade is only foam, shafts and creature highlights (4 % of the screen).
* **Seabed**: a sand strip 24 rows high (6.7 %), boulders in three sizes, an organ-pipe coral and a brain-coral cluster, and kelp in short clumps with gaps (tallest 56 rows = 15.6 % of the height). Kelp fronds are ribbons whose centre line sways between two frames (clean, 0.25 Hz, clumps out of phase).
* **Light**: from the top left everywhere (rock and coral highlights, back of fish lighter than the belly, sheen on the orca's back). Outlines are one texel of the darkest shade.
* **Day / night**: BGP fades chosen from `daylight` (day, dusk drops the lightest shade, night darkens the sea and keeps a faint lit layer); sprites keep their palette, so creatures glow against dark water. `usesWaterColors` is false.

## Sprites (1080p, 3 px per texel)

| creature | art box (texels) | on screen |
|---|---|---|
| fish M (default) / S / L | 12x8 / 10x7 / 14x9 | 36x24 / 30x21 / 42x27 px |
| fry, larva | 8x5, 5x3 | 24x15, 15x9 px |
| angler (stalk + glowing bulb) | 19x14 | 57x42 px (1.5 x fish) |
| orca (white patch, tall fin) | 34x16 | 102x48 px (~3 x fish) |
| jelly (dome, frill, 3 tentacles) | 12x12 | 36x36 px |
| worm | 4x4 | 12x12 px |
| bubbles | 3x3, 4x4 | 9, 12 px |

Size sliders pick the nearest class (fish) or a whole 2x of the class (extreme settings only, never a fractional scale); below the default the big predators keep their drawn size.

## Behaviour

* **Motion**: the simulation ticks at 25 Hz; each sprite glides linearly between ticks and is then rounded to a whole *screen* pixel, so art stays crisp and moves at display rate (no snapping to the texel grid). One `FrameAnimation` drives all sprites and runs only while a glide is in progress and the scene is not paused.
* **Stress** (fish only): from 0.25 a growing share of the school swaps to the inverted OBJ palette (each fish has its own fixed threshold) and trembles by +-1 screen pixel. Above 1.2 the affected fish swap slowly (one palette per 600 ms, a full cycle is 0.83 Hz) with half the school in each phase, so nothing blinks as a whole.
* **Pacing**: tail beat ~0.5-1 Hz, jelly pulse ~3 s, orca slower still, angler lure glow ~5 s; everything is 2-3 drawn frames.
* **Babies** are born as a 5x3 larva that grows through fry to adult size, with a slow two-frame sparkle above them while they spiral out.

## Measured (tests/gameboy-measure.js, run by `npm test` when qml6 is available)

* A calm backdrop: empty sea, 10 s of real time: at most 0.8 % of the screen differs between any two frames 0.1-10 s apart (limit 2 %), fastest texel 0.33 Hz (limit 2 Hz).
* B smooth motion: constant-velocity fish deviate <= 0.65 px from a straight line, whole-pixel positions.
* C eye comfort: lightest shade 4 % of the day screenshot, no stipple window above 20 % isolated texels in the empty sea, progressive stress, +-1 px tremble, alarm swap <= 1 Hz.
* D proportions, seabed band and kelp height as in the table above.

## What changed from the first version

The first version scrolled the water surface and the light rays on an 8 Hz tick, drew the sea as three hard bands joined by 8-px dither ramps with dithered diagonal shafts, snapped sprites to the texel grid at 25 Hz, flipped palette regions on stress and used tall thin kelp, 16x8 fish and fragmentary jellies; it also overlaid an LCD-glass grid. All of that is gone.

## Files

`Scene.qml` (virtual screen + sprite layer), `Ambient.qml` (tile-map background), `GBSprite.qml` / `EntityLayer.qml` (pooled delegates, shared atlas textures, frame loop), `sync.js` (simulation -> sprites, glide), `theme.js` (constants, size classes), `atlas.js` (generated rectangles and layout), `img/` (generated atlases), `preview.png`.

Cost: about 30 static background items, one 500 ms timer (kelp), one `Image` per entity (plus a second only for the lure and sparkle), one frame loop over plain JS state; no layers, shaders or per-entity animations. Everything stops when `paused`.
