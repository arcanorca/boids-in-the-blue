# Amiga theme

A late-80s / early-90s Commodore Amiga (OCS/ECS) look in the spirit of James Pond, Shadow of the Beast and
Deluxe Paint underwater art, redesigned as a **calm wallpaper**: it is looked at for hours, so nothing
flickers, crawls or flashes. The art is generated by `tools/gen-amiga-theme.js` (+ `tools/amiga-backdrop.js`);
`node tools/gen-amiga-theme.js` rewrites `img/`, `--check` verifies it (run by `npm test`), `--sheet out.png
[--scale N]` writes a contact sheet. `preview.png` is the day scene at 1/4 scale (480x270).

## Design principles

1. **Calm first.** The backdrop is static art. The only motion in it: kelp that sways a few whole pixels
   (one 14 s loop, updated 10x/s) and ~20 specks of marine snow drifting down. No colour cycling, no
   full-screen layer that pulses, no stipple. `tests/amiga-measure.js` proves it: with no creatures at most
   2 % of the screen differs between any two frames 0.1-10 s apart and no pixel changes faster than 2 Hz.
2. **Smooth glide, crisp pixels.** Sprites keep nearest-neighbour art but are NOT snapped to the art grid.
   Each sim tick (25 Hz) writes a new target; one shared vsync tween (`EntityLayer.t`, as long as 1.35x the
   smoothed tick interval, so late/early ticks do not stutter) interpolates every sprite, and the sprite lands
   on whole *screen* pixels (`Math.round`), never on texel boundaries. No per-sprite animation objects.
3. **One art-pixel scale.** `vp = round(height / 360)` screen px per art pixel (1080p: 3, i.e. 640x360).
   Every sprite and every piece of scenery uses it. The size sliders pick the nearest hand-drawn class
   (`kinds[].classes`) or an integer magnification `mul` of one; art is never scaled by a fraction.
4. **Eye comfort.** Water = a smooth copper gradient (one colour per scanline, 540 scanlines, 12-bit key
   colours interpolated at 8 bit: plain 12-bit rows band and wobble in hue at 1080p). No full-screen dither of
   any kind. Mean luminance is moderate; creatures are the brightest, most saturated things on screen; the
   seabed, kelp and foreground plants are muted, and foreground plants are dark and slightly translucent.
5. **Craft.** Hand-placed ASCII sprites (`FISH_ART`, `ANGLER_ART`, ...). Outline policy: opaque creatures
   longer than 7 art px get a 1 px outline in a dark shade of their own hue (softer on the lit top edge);
   translucent jellies a bright rim; tiny sprites (newborn fish, worm, bubbles) none. Light comes from above
   (creatures are mirrored when they swim left, so their shading is top-lit and left/right symmetric; scenery
   is lit from the top left). Palette discipline (tested): all sprite and scenery colours are 12-bit, the
   playfield (sand, hills, kelp) uses <= 32 colours, every fish ramp <= 16, no orphan pixels.

## Sizes (1080p, default settings)

fish 12x9 art px = 36x27 px (size variation 10x7 / 14x10 = -17 % / +17 %; newborns 7x5, 5x4), angler 18x14 =
54 px (1.5 fish), orca 37x17 = 111 px (3 fish), jelly bell 12 wide (10-16 by size), worm 3x4 (12 px), bubbles
2-4 art px (6-12 px). Seabed (sand + props) < 6 % of the screen height, tallest kelp 19 %, foreground plants
< 14 %.

## Layers (back to front)

copper lists (night | dusk | day, cross-faded with the day cycle) - static soft light shafts (alpha) - far
hills - hazy back plants - kelp + weed - sand with rocks and corals - marine snow - night shade over the
scenery - **creatures** - foreground plants.
Plants are stacks of 6-row slices shifted sideways by whole screen pixels (0 at the base, up to ~5 px at the
tip): no shear or resampling, which shimmers.

## Creatures

Fish: 5 hand-drawn sizes x 5 colours x 4 tail frames (neutral, up, neutral, down, about one flap per second at
cruising speed; every animation is capped at 3 frames/s); stress adds 2 flush palettes per colour (1/3, 2/3
towards red, mixed in OKLCH and snapped to 12-bit), `red` and `alarm`. Orca (faces left like the simulation's
flag), angler with a lure bulb that changes brightness at ~0.2 Hz and a soft glow, jelly (real alpha, pulse
flattens the bell a row), worm, bubbles, newborn sparkle (a small star in two slow positions).

## Stress and night

- Level 0..1.2: fish swap to pre-coloured palettes and tremble up to one art pixel (a 3 Hz sine, not random
  jumps); the water slowly (1.5 s) fades to a hot list. The swap shows ONE opaque pre-mixed copper list
  (`copper-stress-<base>-<k>.png`) instead of a full-screen blend, so a stressed sea costs no more than a
  calm one once settled.
- Above 1.2 the affected fish alternate slowly between `red` and `alarm` (1 cycle/s, desynchronised per fish);
  the background never flashes.
- Night: the night copper list, plus a navy shade over the lower part of the scenery (soft top edge).

## What changed from the first version

Flicker came from: light shafts and surface ripples colour-cycled at 2.5 fps over large areas, 5 Hz timers
stepping kelp/drift, all sprites snapped to a 4 px grid at 25 Hz (stepped crawl), tail frames driven by speed
at up to 12 frames/s, a 4 Hz sparkle and ~1-2 Hz lure flicker, dithered rays and seams. Proportions came from
per-class bitmaps with unrelated scales (a 78-row near plant, kelp 35 % of the screen, a 7x13 worm). All of
that is gone: static backdrop, one scale, hand-drawn classes, slow animation. A latent bug is also fixed:
`Core.stressLevel()` is not 0 for a calm sim, which tinted the old backdrop permanently.

## Cost

Shared textures, pooled delegates (`EntityLayer.qml`), no per-entity layer/effect/Canvas/timer. Fixed ambient:
1-2 copper images (an opaque list hides the ones below), 1 ray image, ~130 plant slices (bindings update at
10 Hz), 20 specks. Everything stops when `paused`. The one per-frame cost is the shared tween: every sprite
binds `x`/`y` to it.
