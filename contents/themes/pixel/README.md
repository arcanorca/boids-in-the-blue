# Refined Pixel theme (default)

Crisp pixel art in a calm aquarium: a smooth water gradient, soft light shafts, a coral and kelp seabed and a
cast drawn to believable proportions. The art is generated by `tools/gen-pixel-theme.js`;
`node tools/gen-pixel-theme.js` rewrites `img/`, `--check` verifies it (run by `npm test`), `--sheet out.png`
writes a contact sheet of every sprite at 8x, `--scenery out.png` the backdrop textures. `preview.png` is made by
`node tools/gen-previews.js pixel`.

## Design

1. **One art-pixel scale.** `psFor(height)` = `max(2, round(height / 540))` screen px per texel (1080p: 2).
   Every creature and every piece of scenery uses it; the size sliders pick the nearest hand-drawn class
   (`kinds[].classes`) or an integer magnification `mul` of one. Art is never scaled by a fraction.
2. **Proportions (1080p, default sliders).** fish 16x12 texels = 32x24 px (babies 11x8, newborns 8x6), angler
   22x18 = 44x36 px (1.4 fish), orca 60x34 = 120x68 px (3.75 fish), jelly bell 16-24 wide (20 = 40 px) with
   tentacles to 1.8x the bell, worm 4x6 = 8x12 px, bubbles 3-7 texels = 6-14 px. `Scene.qml` sets
   `sim.bodyScale = 16 * ps / 28` (the adult fish is 16 texels wide) and `sim.top = 20 * ps` so creatures stay
   below the surface strip.
3. **Craft.** Silhouettes (body profiles, polygons) and details are placed by hand and rasterised; 3-4 tones per
   colour with the light from the top left, a 1 px outline in a dark shade of the creature's own hue (softer on the
   lit top edge), no orphan texels (tested). Fish have five species with their own markings (bar, freckles,
   lateral line, light back stripe, pale spots). The orca has a blunt, smooth head
   (no mouth line), white chin and belly, eye patch, tall swept dorsal fin, paddle flipper and notched flukes. The pixel orca art
   faces LEFT; the simulation's orca flag is inverted, so `sync.js` flips it exactly like the fish.
4. **Calm.** The backdrop is static art except for a drifting ripple strip under the waterline, two light shafts
   that breathe over about a minute and ~40 specks of marine snow. `tests/amiga-measure.js --theme pixel`: with
   no creatures at most 1 % of the screen differs between any two frames 0.1-10 s apart and no pixel changes
   faster than 0.5 Hz. Everything stops while `paused`.
5. **Smooth glide.** Each sim tick (25 Hz) hands the layer a new target (`EntityLayer.target`); one frame loop
   interpolates every sprite and rounds to whole screen pixels. Delegates are pooled; no per-entity canvas,
   shader or animation object.
6. **Stress.** Above stress 0.25 the fish flush towards red in four pre-baked steps (`<colour>-w1..3`, `red`), above
   1.2 a crimson `alarm` overlay pulses and the fish tremble.

## Layers (back to front)

water gradient (5 stops, day and night sets blended by `daylight`, or the user's two colours) - light shafts -
waterline + ripple strip - `far.png` (distant hills and hazy plants, bluer and dimmer) - `flora.png` (kelp in three
greens, staghorn and sea-fan corals, brain corals, tube sponges, grass; roots are hidden by the sand) - `seabed.png`
(dunes with ripples, rocks, shells, starfish) - marine snow - creatures (jelly, bubble, worm, fish, angler, orca).
The three seabed textures are 600 texels wide and tile; the sand band is 8 % and the tallest plant 21 % of a
1080p screen. At night the seabed fades into the dark with `daylight`.

## Files

`theme.js` (descriptor: arts, classes, lure anchors, water stops), `sync.js` (simulation -> sprites),
`Scene.qml`, `Ambient.qml`, `EntityLayer.qml` (pool + glide), `PixelSprite.qml`, `img/` (generated).
