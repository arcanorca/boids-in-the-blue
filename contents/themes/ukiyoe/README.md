# Ukiyo-e (nishiki-e, Japanese colour woodblock print)

## Why this is a redesign

The first Ukiyo-e theme was the 1-bit theme in another coat: two colours (indigo ink on washi paper) and rows of
horizontal wave strokes. Its water was nothing but lines, its creatures were line drawings, and nothing in it used
what makes a nishiki-e a nishiki-e: flat colour areas, soft bokashi gradients, a repeating traditional pattern and a
small pigment palette. The owner was right that it read as "only lines". This version is built the way a colour
print is built: **flat pigment fills with sumi keyblock outlines, a bokashi sea with the Seigaiha wave-scale
pattern, a calm Hokusai-style line of small rolling crests, Matsushima rocks** and koi in five varieties. There is one colour set
(`PALETTE` in `theme.js`), one texture folder (`img/`) and no ink/paper-only mode any more.

Everything is generated by `tools/gen-ukiyoe-theme.js` (`node tools/gen-ukiyoe-theme.js`; add
`--sheet out.png` for a contact sheet, `SCALE=4`). `theme.js` is the single source of truth for sizes, colours and
anchors. `tests/ukiyoe-theme.test.js` checks the textures; `tests/ukiyoe-measure.js` measures the comfort criteria on
offscreen renders.

## Palette

Twenty traditional pigments (`PALETTE`), muted and harmonious: sumi black `#14161c`, indigo `#0a1a2e`, washi cream
`#e8dfc6` (foam, highlights), beni vermilion `#d95343`, ukon ochre `#d9a441`, malachite `#4f8a5b`, wisteria
`#8a6a9c`, asagi blue-grey, rock browns and sand tones, each with a deep shade. Every opaque texel of every sprite
and the seabed is exactly one of them, and the surface strip is the palette plus its variant's five blues and two creams
(a test checks it). Only the water layers are blends: a smooth bokashi plus the pattern offsets.

## Scale

One texel is a whole number of device pixels: `GRID_ROWS = 360` texel rows (3 px per texel at 1080p). The backdrop
(`Ambient.qml`) is drawn in a texel world; creatures are placed in screen pixels so they glide, but drawn at the same
texel size, nearest-neighbour.

## The wave form: calm and neat

The owner found the waves tiring. A first redesign made the surface line wild (ten crests of very different size with
curling foam claws, a ragged outline), which read as sloppy in the static view, and enlarged and faded the Seigaiha,
which took away its charm. The current form:

- **Surface strip** (`surface-<n>.png`, 640x18 texels = 1920 px at 1080p, tiles horizontally): a low, gentle swell
  (a little under +-1.2 texels) carries twelve small soft crests, one every 53 texels give or take two, 4 to 8
  texels high: a long soft back, a slightly steeper front, a round top. Each crest wears a cream foam cap that
  tapers to nothing on both flanks (a lighter cream on top and a second cream underneath on the larger ones); nothing
  hangs off it: no claws, no spray, no overhang. The line is a smooth function sampled once per column, so every
  slope keeps one steady step rhythm, and every periodic term has a whole number of cycles over the width, so the
  strip wraps without a seam on any screen width. The outline is one indigo texel everywhere (around the silhouette
  and between foam and blue). Under the line run two flat undertow blues that follow it and melt into the sea's top
  blue. The foam is two flat creams (day: the washi pigments; dusk and night: dimmer creams so the white never
  glares). Nothing in the strip moves.
- **Seigaiha**: the classic overlapping fans, scale radius 16 texels (48 px), rings 4 texels, outer arc 2 texels
  in a lighter blue (about 34 levels of luminance by day), fading out at row 210, so the pattern fills the upper
  sea and the lower part is plain bokashi. The tile is 640 texels wide (20 scales), so it repeats without a seam.

## Water: bokashi and Seigaiha

- Three baked 640x300 texel images (`water-0` night, `water-1` dusk, `water-2` day), chosen by daylight in visible
  steps only. Each is a vertical bokashi from a lighter Prussian blue at the surface (`#2f6a96` .. `#1f4f7e` in
  the day) through `#1b4573`, `#153862`, `#0e2a48` to indigo `#0a1a2e` at the floor, in steps of 3 texel rows (a
  step is at most 4 levels per channel, so it never reads as a stripe).
- The upper sea carries the **Seigaiha** (blue ocean waves) pattern: large overlapping concentric wave scales of
  radius 16 texels, rings 4 texels wide in two close blues, the outer arc 2 texels wide and lighter
  (never a 1-texel line). It fades in under the surface strip and is gone by row 210; below that the sea is
  plain bokashi.
- There are no strokes and no dashes anywhere in the water.

## Surface

`surface-<n>.png` (640x18, one per daylight variant): the crest line described above, painted in five flat blues
(deep, mid, the sea's own top blue, the pale sky) plus two creams per variant, with indigo keyblock outlines. The
strip sits at texel row 18 (54 px), so the first drawn pixel is below the 54 px top panel. It ends at row 36, where the
Seigaiha starts. The simulation keeps creature centres below texel row 44 (`SURFACE.floor`, `sim.top`), so a fish never
swims over the crests.

## Seabed and kelp

- `seabed.png` (640x32 = 8.9 % of the height): sand dunes in sand-shade and cream-ochre with printed wave contours
  that follow the dune (continuous bands), brown rock islands with a lit ochre crescent and a dark brown outline,
  Matsushima pine tufts on top, vermilion staghorn coral and starfish.
- Nine wakame kelp plants (3 height classes, tallest 17.8 % of the height): a stipe opening into a broad ruffled
  malachite blade with darker midrib and herringbone veins. They sway by whole texels, one frame every 2 s.

## Creatures (1-texel sumi outlines, flat fills)

- **Koi**: the five simulation colours are five varieties, different in colour and in pattern: kohaku (white with red
  head cap and saddle), ogon (plain gold, darker back), sanke (white, red, sumi black accents), asagi (blue-grey with
  a net of darker scales, ochre belly), shusui (blue back, white belly, red cheek). Five size classes; the reference
  mid fish is 13x9 texels, 39x27 px at 1080p.
- **Orca**: sumi black with cream eye patch, saddle, belly and throat, a slate rim light so it reads on blue; 40x23
  texels (120x69 px, about three fish long). The orca flag is inverted in the simulation, so the sprite uses
  `ent.facingRight` as is (tested).
- **Angler**: deep wisteria with cream teeth, an ochre eye and a vermilion lantern lure (three slow levels).
- **Jelly**: pale wisteria bell with wisteria canals, cream frill and tentacles. **Worm**: vermilion. **Bubbles**:
  cream rings, 6 and 12 px.

## Motion, day/night, stress

- Everything but kelp (0.5 Hz) and the sprite frame sets is static. Creatures glide at display rate and land on
  whole screen pixels. The 500 ms backdrop timer only advances kelp frames and stops on `paused`.
- Night: the night water and surface variants are darker indigo blues; an indigo veil (opacity steps with daylight)
  sinks the seabed and kelp. Creatures keep their colours.
- Stress: from 0.25 a growing share of the fish turns into the alarm variant (all vermilion with deep vermilion
  markings); from 0.5 a share trembles by one texel; above 1.2 about a third alternate between alarm and normal at
  0.8 Hz. Nothing ever flashes across the screen.

## Measured (1920x1080, default settings, `node tests/ukiyoe-measure.js`)

- Calm backdrop: at most 0.70 % of pixels differ between frames 0.1-10 s apart (limit 2 %, only the kelp moves); the
  fastest pixel changes 0.49 times per second (limit 2 Hz). The wave strip and the Seigaiha are static.
- Glide: residual of on-screen x against a straight line 0.67 px (limit 1); sprites land on whole screen pixels.
- Mean luminance (gamma-encoded): day 25.5 % (was 25.8 % with the previous waves), crowded 26.7 %, feeding 25.8 %,
  stress 25.3-25.4 %, night 15.6 %. The limit for this theme is 28 % (25 % for the one-colour themes) and the day scene
  must not be brighter than the previous design (checked).
- Busy 24x24 px windows (period <= 2 texels) in the creature-free backdrop: none flagged (worst 45 %, flag at 50 %).
  Surface strip: no 24 px window at or above 50 %, one-column zigzags in the wave line 12 at most, no foam piece under 8 texels.
- Sizes: fish 39x27 px, orca 3.08 fish lengths, angler 1.46, jelly 1.08, worm 0.31; seabed 8.9 %, tallest kelp 17.8 %.
- Stress 1.5: at most 1.63 alarm/normal flips per second per fish (limit 2); tremble at most 2.13 changes per second
  (limit 4).
