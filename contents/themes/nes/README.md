# NES theme

The aquarium drawn the way a Nintendo Entertainment System (2C02 PPU) game would draw it: an
underwater level in the spirit of Super Mario Bros. world 2-2, Mega Man 2's Bubble Man stage or
Capcom's Little Mermaid. The art is generated by `tools/gen-nes-theme.js` from 2-bit tile data;
`theme.js` is the single source of truth for palettes, sizes and pacing.

## Hardware constraints that are emulated

| NES | Here |
|---|---|
| 64-colour master palette | The classic NTSC set that FCEUX ships and the NESdev wiki lists (not FBX Smooth, not Nestopia's YUV). Every pixel of every PNG is one of its entries; `tests/nes-theme.test.js` checks it. |
| 2-bit tiles, colour 0 transparent | All art is authored as indices 0..3 in 8x8 tiles. The PNGs are produced afterwards by mapping indices through a palette. |
| 4 sprite palettes, 3 colours each | Shared by every creature (table below). |
| Metasprites of 8x8 tiles, one palette per sprite | A creature is a grid of whole tiles; the generator cuts each into tiles, folds mirrored duplicates (horizontal flip attribute) and fails if a part leaves the tile grid. 58 unique sprite tiles are used (limit 256). |
| 4 background palettes, one per 16x16 area | The seabed, waterline and strips are painted into palette-tagged cells; the generator fails when a 16x16 area mixes palettes. Plants and boulders therefore claim whole 16-px columns, the same layout pressure real NES levels had. |
| One universal backdrop colour | Deepest water. It is the "transparent" colour of every background tile, so the seabed art sits on it. |
| Background tiles cannot flip | Background tiles are counted without flip folding: about 214 unique tiles per CHR bank (limit 256). The kelp sway is a CHR bank switch (two banks, 1 s apart). |
| Low internal resolution | One virtual pixel = `floor(height / 240)` screen pixels (at least 2): 1080p is 480x270 at 4x, 1440p 427x240 at 6x. Sprites snap to the virtual grid. |
| 2-frame animation | Fish, orca, angler, jelly and worm each have two frames chosen by the sim state; the angler's lure has three tile variants. |
| No alpha | Nothing is translucent: jellies are opaque tiles, a dying worm blinks, a newborn fish gets a twinkling sparkle sprite. |
| Palette fades for night stages | Day, dusk and night are three background palette sets. Dusk rotates the water palette one shade deeper plus the NES "subtract one brightness row" fade for the others; night is hand-picked. Sprite palettes stay (as in games that only fade the playfield). |
| Horizontal scroll | The waterline nametable (256 px) scrolls one virtual pixel per tick of an 8 Hz clock. |
| Not emulated | The 64-sprite / 8-per-scanline limits and the flicker they cause (not wanted on a wallpaper), mid-frame palette changes, NTSC artefacts. |

## Palettes

Sprite palettes (colours 1..3; `theme.js` SPR_PAL):

| # | name | colours | used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | sea | $0F $21 $30 (black, sky blue, white) | orca, aqua fish, bubbles, sparkle |
| 1 | sun | $16 $27 $38 (red-orange, orange, yellow) | coral / amber / lemon fish, worm, angler lure |
| 2 | alarm | $0F $15 $35 (black, crimson, pink) | stressed fish only |
| 3 | violet | $03 $23 $33 (indigo, periwinkle, lavender) | orchid fish, angler, jelly |

The five fish colours fit the four palettes by reusing `sun` with different index arrangements and
markings: coral has a red-orange body with a yellow eye and orange bars, amber an orange body with
red-orange freckles, lemon a yellow body with orange outline and lateral line. Aqua and orchid use
their own palette and a different marking (back stripe / spots).

Background palettes, day (universal colour $02): water $21 $11 $12, sand $18 $27 $37, kelp $0A $1A
$2A, foam $21 $31 $30. The water is three solid bands joined by two-row ordered-dither strips (25 % /
75 %), the NES way of blending two colours.

## Stress

Each fish has a fixed individual threshold. As stress rises more fish get the `alarm` palette (an OAM
palette attribute swap: 0 % at 0.25 up to 100 % at 1.2); those fish also shake by whole virtual pixels
(at most 2) at about 8 Hz. Above 1.2 the alarm palette itself cycles between two states
($0F $15 $35 and $0F $06 $16) every 400 ms (1.25 Hz), touching only the stressed sprites; nothing
flashes full screen.

## Files

* `theme.js` descriptor: palettes, sizes, pacing, layout helpers
* `Scene.qml`, `Ambient.qml` (background, 8 Hz clock), `EntityLayer.qml` (pool), `NesSprite.qml`, `sync.js`
